## Description of the project

**Problem.** A human player beats a new souls-like boss after a handful of deaths: they learn its moveset, read each attack's wind-up ("telegraph"), dodge on the right frame, and punish during recovery. They also carry concepts like "delayed overhead slam" or "three-hit combo, roll on the third" to the next boss and the next game. Deep RL does not do this. In the recent Dark Souls learning environment DSLE (2026), PPO and DQN trained from pixels for tens of hours won at most 0.33% of fights against the tutorial boss and 0% against the others. We ask whether the course's approach, building a probabilistic model of the world and inverting it with sampling, closes this gap.

**Environment and data.** Real souls-like games need a Windows machine running the game in real time, which makes them too slow to train on a laptop and mixes the cognitive question with vision. We will build **MiniSouls**, a lightweight 2D arena in Python/NumPy (thousands of steps per second on a CPU) with the core souls-like mechanics: stamina, dodge with invincibility frames, block, attack, limited heals, and boss phases. Each boss is generated by a **probabilistic program**: it composes moves from a library of attack primitives (each with a telegraph cue, wind-up time, active frames, range, damage and recovery) and chains them with a stochastic, state-dependent behavior policy (a Markov chain over moves conditioned on distance, last move and HP phase). This gives an unlimited, controllable dataset: ~40 training bosses and held-out test bosses with (a) new timings of known moves, (b) new combinations of known moves, (c) a never-seen move, and (d) changed game rules (different dodge or stamina rules) standing in for "a different souls-like game." No external data is needed.

**Model.** Our agent keeps an explicit generative model of the boss written in a probabilistic programming language (NumPyro). During a fight it runs a **particle filter (SMC)** over the boss's current move and time-in-move to predict when the next hit lands, and uses **MCMC rejuvenation** (resample-move) to update its beliefs about the boss's static parameters (timings, move transition probabilities). Recognizing a move from a few frames of telegraph is **Bayesian concept learning** with the size principle; a Chinese-restaurant-process prior lets the agent posit a new move when none in its library explains the data. A **hierarchical prior learned across training bosses** (learning to learn) makes the next boss cheaper. Actions are chosen by planning inside sampled boss futures (expected utility over particles). We compare against PPO (with and without an LSTM memory) and a meta-RL baseline, and ablate the posterior to a single best guess (MAP) to test whether averaging beats committing.

**Relation to the course.** The project is a direct application of the lectures: Bayesian concept learning and the size principle (Lec 3–5; Tenenbaum, Xu & Tenenbaum), Bayes nets and probabilistic programs as world models (Lec 2, 6–7), exact inference on the move HMM as a reference (Lec 8), importance sampling, MCMC and SMC as the agent's inference engine (Lec 9–12; Saad et al. SMC learning), and the Lake et al. "Building Machines" ingredients: compositional primitives reused across bosses as in Bayesian Program Learning, intuitive models of other agents, learning-to-learn, and the Frostbite/EMPA comparison of model-based versus model-free sample efficiency (Tsividis et al. 2021).

# Part B: the full plan

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Treat each boss as a small **program** that generates its behavior. A player who "learns a boss" is doing **program induction**: they infer which moves it has, what the telegraphs look like, how long each wind-up lasts, and which move follows which. A player who "reads an attack" mid-fight is doing **online inference** inside that program. A veteran who learns the next boss faster has a **prior over boss programs** learned from earlier bosses. Each of those three is a course topic (concept learning, SMC filtering, hierarchical Bayes / learning to learn), and together they give a concrete prediction: a Bayesian agent should need deaths, not millions of frames, and should transfer moves across bosses compositionally.

## 2. What has been done recently

**Real souls-like games as RL environments.**

- **DSLE (Gezgin et al., Aug 2026, [arXiv 2608.09902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09902))**: a containerized Gymnasium environment for all 22 boss fights in *Dark Souls: Remastered*, with a 5-boss subset DSLE-5. Pixel observations, real-time actions. PPO and DQN reached at most 0.33% wins on the tutorial boss and 0% elsewhere; an expert system and an evolutionary baseline beat only the tutorial boss (63% and 43% peak). Runs take tens of wall-clock hours. This is the headline motivation: standard deep RL essentially fails.
- **SoulsGym ([github.com/amacati/SoulsGym](https://github.com/amacati/SoulsGym))**: a Gymnasium extension for *Dark Souls III* that reads game memory to give ground-truth state (positions, animation timings). One boss (Iudex Gundyr) is implemented and "solved" at a 45% win rate. Windows-only, needs the purchased game running. Pixel-based training is an open roadmap item.
- Takeaways: (1) with ground-truth state, RL can learn one boss; from pixels, it can't; (2) nobody reports transfer across bosses; (3) the real games are too slow and too heavy for a laptop project.

**Model-based and theory-based agents (course and frontier).**

- **EMPA, Tsividis et al. 2021 (course reading)**: an agent that learns object-oriented "theories" of VGDL games by Bayesian inference and plans in them, reaching human-like sample efficiency where DDQN needed orders of magnitude more data. Our project is EMPA's idea moved to real-time, adversarial, stochastic combat.
- **WorldCoder (Tang et al. 2024)** and **PoE-World (Piriyakulkij et al. 2025)**: LLMs write executable world models (PoE-World as products of small programmatic experts) for Atari-like games. Both keep a single best model, not a weighted posterior.
- **AutumnBench (Warrier et al. 2025)**: LLM agents explore poorly and cling to rules after the evidence contradicts them; humans spend far more actions testing hypotheses. **ARC-AGI-3 (2026)**: frontier agents now beat interactive puzzle games by writing compact world models, but in deterministic, turn-based worlds. Souls-like fights are the opposite: stochastic, real-time, adversarial.
- **DreamerV3 (Hafner et al.)** and **DIAMOND (Alonso et al. 2024)**: neural world models that learn from pixels; strong, but data- and GPU-hungry, and their latents are not interpretable as "moves."

**Inferring what the opponent is.**

- **Bayesian Policy Reuse (Rosman et al. 2016)**: keep a belief over which known task you are facing and pick the matching policy; the closest classical ancestor of "which boss / which move is this."
- **Opponent modeling survey (Nashed & Zilberstein 2022, JAIR)**: Bayesian type-based opponent models are standard in games, but almost always for turn-based or strategic play, not frame-level combat timing.
- **VariBAD (Zintgraf et al. 2020)** and RL²: meta-RL that learns an amortized belief over the task. These are the right neural baselines: they learn to adapt, but their belief is a black-box vector rather than a structured program.

**Gap this project fills (my inference from the above):** no work combines (a) an explicit compositional generative model of a real-time opponent, (b) particle-based online inference to time defensive actions, and (c) a learned prior over opponents, and tests few-shot learning and transfer against deep RL in a souls-like setting.

## 3. Why a proxy environment, and why this one

| Option | Laptop-trainable? | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Real game (DSLE, SoulsGym) | No | Real-time, Windows + game license, tens of hours per run, 0% baseline results, perception dominates |
| Pixel-based clone (e.g., a 3D Unity arena) | Barely | Vision and rendering cost; still mixes perception with the cognitive question |
| **MiniSouls: 2D arena, symbolic observations** | **Yes** | Abstracts away graphics, which we justify below |

The justification: the cognitive skill we study is **learning and predicting an opponent's behavior from few encounters**, not visual recognition. A human also sees a telegraph (an animation) as a discrete cue plus its timing; MiniSouls gives the agent exactly that: a noisy cue token for the boss's current animation, the boss's position and facing, and the clock. Keeping the cue *noisy and partially ambiguous* (several moves share similar wind-ups early on; feints exist) keeps the inference problem real. The fidelity we keep is the part that makes souls-likes hard: frame-tight dodge windows, stamina economy, delayed attacks, combos, and phase changes.

Optional bridge, only if time and a Windows machine allow: run the trained boss-model inference *read-only* on SoulsGym's ground-truth state for Iudex Gundyr to predict attack timing. This is not required for the project.

## 4. MiniSouls environment spec

**Arena.** Continuous 2D plane (or a 1D lane for the first version), 20 decisions per second of game time, episodes end on player or boss death or a time limit.

**Player.** HP, stamina (regenerates), 6 actions: move (4 directions or approach/retreat), light attack, heavy attack, dodge-roll (fixed i-frame window, stamina cost), block (stamina drain on hit), heal (3 charges, slow, punishable), idle.

**Boss generative program** (this is both the data generator and, in a parametric form, the agent's model):

```
boss ~ BossPrior(θ_global):
  K ~ 3 + Poisson(2)                      # number of moves
  for each move k:
      primitive_k ~ Categorical(library)   # slam, sweep, lunge, combo-n, AoE, grab, projectile, delayed slam, feint
      windup_k ~ LogNormal(μ_prim, σ)      # telegraph duration
      active_k, range_k, damage_k, recovery_k ~ priors(primitive_k)
      cue_k  = cue(primitive_k) + noise    # what the player sees
  T[last_move, distance_bin, phase] ~ Dirichlet(α)   # behavior policy
  phase-2 at 50% HP: speed-up factor and possibly a new move
```

**Splits.** 40 training bosses; 4 held-out test sets of 10 bosses each:
- **T1 interpolation:** known primitives, new timings.
- **T2 composition:** known primitives in new combinations and new transition structure.
- **T3 novelty:** one never-seen primitive.
- **T4 "other game":** changed rules (shorter i-frames, costlier stamina, faster heals), either given as a context vector or to be inferred. This is the "generalize between souls-likes with appropriate context and controls" part.

Seeds are fixed and the generator is versioned, so the dataset is reproducible.

## 5. The model

### 5.1 Generative model (Bayes net / probabilistic program)

Per time step *t* the boss has a hidden state *z_t* = (current move *m_t*, time-in-move *τ_t*). The agent sees *o_t* = (noisy cue, boss position, boss facing). As a dynamic Bayes net:

```
θ_global ──► θ_boss ──► T (move transitions), {windup_k, active_k, ...}
                          │
   m_{t-1}, τ_{t-1}, d_t ─┴─► m_t, τ_t ──► cue_t, hitbox_t ──► damage to player
```

This is a hidden semi-Markov model whose emission and duration parameters are tied to a compositional move library. Writing it in NumPyro (or a 200-line hand-written PPL) makes the course's PPL lectures concrete.

### 5.2 Inference, at three time scales

1. **Within a fight (milliseconds): SMC particle filter** over *z_t*, with 200–500 particles. Each particle predicts *when* and *where* the next hit lands; the predictive distribution drives the dodge decision. Reference: exact forward filtering on the discrete HMM version (Lec 8) gives ground truth to check the filter against.
2. **Across attempts on one boss: resample-move SMC / MCMC rejuvenation** over *θ_boss* (timings, transition matrix, which primitives the boss has). Metropolis–Hastings and Gibbs moves (Lec 10–11), following the SMC-learning recipe of Saad et al. 2023. Importance sampling (Lec 9) serves as a simple baseline for this level.
3. **Across bosses: hierarchical learning of θ_global** (typical timings per primitive, which primitives co-occur, how phase 2 changes things). "Learning is inference one level up."

**Move recognition as concept learning.** Given the first few frames of a wind-up, the agent infers which move it is. With hypotheses as move concepts, the **size principle** favors moves whose timing distribution is tight and consistent with what was seen (Tenenbaum 1999; Xu & Tenenbaum 2007). A **Chinese restaurant process** over moves lets the agent create a new move concept when existing ones explain the data badly (test set T3).

### 5.3 Acting

Model-predictive control over particles: for each candidate action, simulate the next ~1–2 seconds under a sample of particles and pick the action with the highest expected return (survival, damage dealt, stamina). Early in a new fight the agent can value **information** (stay at mid-range and bait moves to see them), which lets us ask whether it explores like a player does. A small learned "reflex" network distilled from the planner is a stretch goal (an amortized fast path, the System 1 / System 2 story).

## 6. Baselines and ablations

| Agent | What it tests |
|---|---|
| PPO (Stable-Baselines3), MLP on symbolic obs | Standard model-free RL |
| PPO + LSTM / frame stack | Is memory alone enough? |
| Meta-RL (RL² or VariBAD-lite) trained across the 40 bosses | Amortized, black-box Bayes-adaptive learning |
| Scripted "veteran" heuristic (dodge on any wind-up) | Sanity floor/ceiling |
| **Ours: Bayesian boss model + SMC + MPC** | The full cognitive agent |
| Ours, **MAP lesion** (1 particle / single best hypothesis) | "Averaging beats committing" (motif M2) |
| Ours, **flat prior** (no learned θ_global) | Value of learning to learn |
| Ours, **non-compositional** (each boss's moves learned from scratch) | Value of a reusable move library (BPL-style) |
| Ours, **no CRP** (fixed library) | Handling genuinely novel moves |

## 7. Experiments, metrics and hypotheses

**Metrics.** (1) **Deaths to first win** and win rate after *n* attempts on held-out bosses: the souls-like learning curve. (2) Environment steps and CPU time to reach a given win rate. (3) Predictive log-likelihood and calibration of the next move and hit timing. (4) Transfer: performance on T1–T4 relative to training bosses.

**Hypotheses.**
- H1 (sample efficiency): the Bayesian agent beats its first held-out boss within ~10 attempts; PPO needs orders of magnitude more steps (an EMPA/Frostbite-style gap).
- H2 (composition): the advantage is largest on T2 (new combinations), because moves are reused as units.
- H3 (averaging): the MAP lesion dies more to delayed attacks and feints, where hedging over two hypotheses matters.
- H4 (novelty and rules): with the CRP and an inferred rule context, performance degrades gracefully on T3 and T4; meta-RL degrades sharply.
- Optional human comparison: two or three friends play a few held-out bosses in a simple pygame front end; compare deaths-to-first-win curves with the agent's.

## 9. Laptop compute budget

- MiniSouls step: pure NumPy, target over 5,000 steps/s on one core. A 90-second fight is ~1,800 steps.
- PPO baselines: 2–10 M steps, about 1–3 hours each on a laptop CPU; meta-RL somewhat more, with fewer seeds.
- Bayesian agent: 300 particles × 20 decisions/s × short MPC rollouts, vectorized in NumPy/JAX, should run faster than real time. No training run in the usual sense; "learning" is posterior updates between attempts plus fitting θ_global over 40 training bosses (minutes).
- No GPU needed. Everything fits in under ~20 CPU-hours total, including 3–5 seeds per baseline.

## 11. Risks and fallbacks

- **Planning is too slow or too myopic** → restrict to a 1D lane first; plan only over {dodge now, dodge later, attack, back off}; distil to a reflex policy.
- **SMC degeneracy over long fights** → resample-move with MCMC rejuvenation, which is exactly what Lec 11–12 cover; fall back to exact filtering on the discrete version.
- **PPO turns out to be strong in the proxy** → that is still a result; the comparison then shifts to steps-to-competence and transfer on T2–T4, where a fixed policy should struggle.
- **Scope creep** → the minimum viable project is: env + SMC move-prediction agent + PPO baseline + MAP ablation on T1/T2. Everything else is a stretch.

## 12. Stretch goals

- An LLM proposes new move primitives in the PPL from fight logs (the "proposals are the bottleneck" motif), with SMC weighting them.

- A read-only bridge to SoulsGym's ground-truth state to predict Iudex Gundyr's attack timing.
